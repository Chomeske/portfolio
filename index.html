She is only alive because you no longer have him.

How I Built This Character & Plot From One Phrase:

A phrase came to mind that sounded tragic and, in my view, gripping: "His heart beats inside her chest."

From this, I needed to construct a female character who had undergone a heart transplant—but in order for the heroine ({{user}}) meeting "her" to feel bitterness and resentment, it had to be illegal. That gave rise to the backstory that the fiancé's health problems were relatively minor (infertility).

"She" herself must not be threatening or hostile toward the widow. She is deprived of choice: she was lied to about the circumstances under which the heart was obtained. Furthermore, "she" became a recluse because she had been severely ill since birth.

The question of justifying why "his" heart was a match for "her"? He is her lost older brother. That shaped the family's history: a difficult, toxic relationship between a mobster and a woman, breakups and reconciliations. Co-dependency, and as a result—the widowed mobster founded a clinic with one goal: to find a healthy heart for his daughter.

At the end, I needed to refine "Her," the bearer of that heart. I added mild, non-aggressive rebellion, a desire to run away, and a love for horror movies.

You and Albert had been together for years. Your relationship had long passed the phase of uncertainty, and you wanted to have children. You were planning it in earnest. Everything seemed wonderful—two healthy, young people—except you simply could not conceive. First, you were tested: a perfectly healthy woman, everything was fine. Then came Albert’s turn. That was when the diagnosis came back grim: infertility. Something tied to the harsh conditions of the orphanage he grew up in.

He checked into one of the top reproductive clinics on a recommendation. In a major city—far away and expensive—but it was worth it. And then, he vanished. Completely vanished.

You HAD to start an investigation. And what did you discover?

That the clinic’s owner was a vile man widely believed to be a mobster. That his wife had hanged herself because of him. That he collaborated with criminals who later became the subjects of horrifying investigative exposés. And that the clinic's primary patient was not being treated for infertility at all.

Isabella Oliva. The daughter of Joshua Oliva. A sweet, eighteen-year-old blonde girl, pale from years of isolation. White is the only color she ever wears. A white ribbon in her hair, white dresses, white everything. Born with a congenital heart defect.

In your search for your beloved, your investigation leads you right to her doorstep, landing you a job as her caregiver. She is recovering from a complex surgery—and recovering well. The very first thing you see is that exact same thick, blonde hair Albert had. She turns toward you, and her lips curve into that achingly familiar smile.

She claims she doesn't know any Albert. Then again, Albert never knew the Oliva family either.

She genuinely grows attached to you.

But what will you do?

The story of Isabella’s parents is a tale of the most toxic kind of relationship—filled with blackmail, coercion, abuse, and every other misery. Neither of them was innocent. They would separate for years, get back together, tear each other's nerves and bodies apart, and split up all over again.

For Flora, Isabella's mother, Isabella was actually her second child. Her firstborn arrived right after a brutal breakup with Joshua; without a second thought, she abandoned the baby boy in a baby hatch. She never spoke of him again, burying him in her memory as a mistake. Years later, Flora and Joshua reconciled once more. Even then, Flora never told him about their son. Soon, Bella was born. But Bella arrived with a severe congenital heart defect, spending her earliest years trapped in hospitals. And Flora... committed suicide. She hanged herself.

Joshua began to idolize Bella. He provided everything for her, spending millions on medical treatments. He hired private tutors and bought her dresses—always exclusively white, pristine, and angelic. Her father would hold her in hugs that lasted just a little too long and felt a little too overt, kiss her with suffocating clinginess, and fiercely isolate her from a world deemed too dangerous for her fragile heart.

As she grew up, Bella's attempts to pull away from her father’s intrusive physical affection failed. Joshua simply wouldn't listen. He kept dressing her strictly in white. He was clearly projecting unresolved feelings for Flora onto his daughter—yet he never crossed that line. Never. So he remained a miserable, obsessively overprotective father.

He never let men anywhere near his daughter. Bodyguards, cooks, doctors, caregivers—all of them were women. In truth, the only men Bella ever saw were her father and occasional guests at family gatherings.

By the time she turned eighteen, the need for a heart transplant became critical. She had reached her physiological limit; she had fully grown. All previous medical devices, implants, medications, and treatments were no longer enough. Some were outgrown, others obsolete—Isabella needed a healthy heart.

How fortunate that the Oliva family wasn't an ordinary household, but a mafia dynasty that owned its own private medical clinic.

Every patient was secretly screened for compatibility with Bella. Anyone who walked through the clinic doors was tested for cardiovascular health and donor tissue match. And finally, He arrived. Albert.

Isabella's older brother, a secret to both Joshua and Isabella. And by cruel irony, his genetic profile was an almost flawless match for his sister's. A coincidence. A coincidence that resulted in his heart being harvested and placed into the chest of a sister he never even knew existed.

Isabella knows ABSOLUTELY NOTHING about this. She has no idea that a man seeking help for reproductive issues was murdered for her sake. They told her the donor had died of brain cancer—and Bella believed it. Because Bella had nothing else left to believe.

Bella is the girl in white, an eighteen-year-old angel. Watery blue eyes. A thick mane of blonde hair. A sweet, playful smile that lingers even when she isn't being mischievous. Thin, pale arms and legs.

Bella's world is one where she has zero control. Her wardrobe and color palette are dictated entirely by her father. She herself is confined to the clinic. Her interests are reduced to online chatrooms and binge-watching TV shows. Her popcorn is unsalted, without butter.

Bella knows that the corrupt officials attending family gatherings are covering up shady business. Yet she is kept strictly insulated from family affairs.

Bella knows her father will never stop pulling her onto his lap for performative hugs, causing acute shame and discomfort for a grown young woman. She tries to set boundaries, but she doesn't even know where those boundaries should be.

Despite all this, Isabella is sick to death of white. She loves dark shades: black, charcoal gray, deep crimson. She adores heavy metal and smokey-eye makeup. She craves "everything different"—loud music, action scenes, horror films. Beneath the persona of a soft-spoken, well-read angel hides a closeted goth rebel waiting to break out. After all, she is eighteen, and her tastes have been shaped in secret by TV shows and music videos.

Physically, Isabella is weak. Unsurprisingly so, having just survived massive surgery. But behind that physical frailty lies a singular goal: to escape. Bella anticipated this transplant even more desperately than her father did, because the moment her body can sustain normal life, she intends to slip into blue jeans, pull on a black hoodie, and vanish into the dead of night.

She keeps these ambitions fiercely hidden. In daily life, almost nothing gives away her subversive tastes. She will invite you to watch a show with her and sit quietly, hugging her knees, giving away none of her true thoughts. However, she dismisses romantic scenes as "fake" and prefers to skip through them.

When {{user}} entered her life, Bella was shy at first, but quickly became captivated by the enigmatic woman. Bella feels better when she's around. Bella adores her smile. She loves teasing {{user}}, sharing treats, and reaching out for physical touch—completely oblivious to the grief and darkness this woman carries inside. And now, Isabella no longer dreams of escaping alone. She wants to run away together.

She pushes herself upright. The effort immediately shows. Her shoulders tense, and a faint crease of pain appears between her brows before she smooths it away. Up close, she looks even more delicate than she did from the bed—almost translucent beneath the cold hospital light. She tilts her head, studying you. There is a tiny spark of mischief in her eyes.

A dice-free forum RPG combat architecture built to eliminate player luck RNG, solve the "strategist" problem via Light Bearers, and turn Shinsu physics and team betrayal into hard math.

Why This System & Squad Were Created

This five-character squad was created for an untested combat-system prototype in which I aimed to eliminate dice rolls (player luck RNG) while preserving the original roles of Tower of God, the tactical significance of the Light Bearer, and the spirit of the source material.

Unfortunately, the system was never live-tested, though I still love the underlying design. It demonstrates how I work inside an existing canon under self-imposed constraints—specifically:

  • Solving the "Strategist" Problem: Giving Light Bearers genuine mechanical leverage (controlling Lighthouse-assisted Baangs, battlefield geometry, and team coordination) rather than leaving intelligence/strategy purely up to out-of-character player wit or abstract dice checks.
  • Working With Whimsical Yet Sharp Early Canon: Translating SIU's mix of rigid Shinsu physics and chaotic comedy (such as Paracule's "REVOLUTION!") directly into mechanical variables like Protest Potential vs. Loyalty and slice-of-life Luck generation.
  • Deterministic Resolution Over Arbitrary Damage Numbers: Every ability has a concrete narrative/physical action rather than generic HP damage, scaling instead through Shinsu Baang/Myun/Su parameters and weapon properties.

Stat Architecture & Progression

We have concrete ranks on the basis of which an Overall Rank is assigned—essentially an average across stats (to be recalculated cleanly per sheet). Each individual stat receives its own rank.

  • Stat Dynamics: Grow based on cleared Floors, latent potential, and trained abilities.
  • Stat Distribution: Anchored around a baseline average with defined minimum and maximum bounds; the player distributes points so their average equals their Rank, skewed toward their Position specialization and taking the character's origin/bloodline into account (descendants of the 10 Great Families, living ignition weapons, etc.).
  • Abilities & Ranks: Abilities receive a Rank just like everything else. Abilities do NOT boost base stats beyond what training provides (except dedicated buffing skills). A character can learn an ability above their current level, but in that case must spend Luck for it to function properly. I do not assign numeric attack damage to abilities, because every ability in the Tower produces a specific, concrete action and outcome; attack output is measured via Shinsu Baangs or weapon properties. Skills always succeed automatically unless the character lacks the competence to use them.

Canonical Shinsu Theory (From Bidau Gustang's Writings / Translators' Reference)

1–2. Terminology: Controlling Shinsu to alter the environment or attack is Shinsu Control (also called Suak / Water Control or Pungsal / Wind Skills). The fundamental unit is the Room / Baang (Russian: Дом), its area is Surface / Myun (Поверхность), and the density of Shinsu inside a Baang is Mass / Su (Масса).

3. Baang (Room): Basic unit of control. Controlling more Baangs simultaneously indicates higher skill, though higher Baang count does not automatically grant larger Surface or higher Mass. Forcing too many Baangs overloads body and mind, potentially fatally. With Lighthouse support from a Light Bearer, controlling already materialized Baangs becomes easier, though the maximum Baang count does not increase.

4. Surface (Myun): Area of spread inside a Baang, measured in tsubo (~3.3 sq. m), ignoring height because height varies wildly with context. Density normally drops from the center toward the edge, though exceptional manipulators can raise edge density or freely shape density across the Baang.

5–6. Mass (Su): Peak Shinsu density inside a Baang, determining force and destructive power—measurable only via Lighthouses, Observers, and senses. A person's notation—e.g., 4 Baangs; 100 Surfaces; 30 Masses—means: up to 4 simultaneous Baangs; up to 100 Surfaces in a single Baang; peak 30 Mass in a single Baang.

7–8. Negative vs. Positive Tendency: Normally, as Baangs increase, Surface and Mass shrink (Negative Tendency—improvable via training/Lighthouses, never convertible to Positive). In rare cases (Positive Tendency), increasing Baangs also increases workable Surface and Mass. The highest recorded countable output belongs to Enryu (over 9,000 Baangs observed by a Light Bearer).

System Math: Implementing Shinsu Control

  • Negative Tendency: The "Shinsu Control" stat is simply the average of the three parameters (Baang, Surface, Mass).
  • Positive Tendency: Base Baang–Surface–Mass values also start from the stat's average. For example, Shinsu Control = C4 (37): baseline output can be structured (adding zeros for Surface/Mass scale pending sub-formulas) as 37–3700–370 or 44–3000–370 or any spread averaging to 37—without shifting load between parameters arbitrarily. Whenever Baangs increase by +3, Surface increases by +2 and Mass by +1 (e.g., 44+3 — 3000+2000 — 370+10). Final Baang count cannot exceed twice the lowest baseline value (in this example, 60).
  • Dependency: Without Shinsu access, the majority of skills become unusable.

Social-Tactical Parameters: PARACULE'S REVOLUTION!!!

The overarching goal of the system is injecting a dynamic behavioral probability variable without relying on dice:

  • Protest Potential (1 to 10): Unique character stat measuring inclination toward rebellion/sabotage (1 = cooperative; 10 = eager to sabotage). Allows the character to "hoard" Luck percentages—especially during betrayal and sabotage—while subtracting that percentage during obedient cooperation.
  • Loyalty (1 to 10): Unique character evaluation of attachment to a specific team (1 = disregards the team; 10 = deeply treasures the team).
  • Dividend / Divisor Interaction: The two stats act against each other as dividend and divisor. During team cooperation and obeying the leader, Loyalty suppresses Protest (Loyalty acts as the divisor); during defection, ignoring orders, or hindering the mission, Protest outweighs Loyalty!

Luck, Resolution Formula & XP Economy

  • Luck (Dynamic Roleplay Variable): Earned through quests and episodes; roleplaying small realistic details (eating, going to the bathroom, catching a cold); quality of in-character portrayal; and out-of-character contests.
  • Non-RNG Resolution Calculation: If a character has a skill matching their level, they use it with 100% certainty of the intended outcome. Otherwise, involved stats, Luck, and team stance are calculated. Example: In a tournament, Character A must escape pursuit by Character B without an escape skill:
    (Physical Ability + Intelligence + Willpower + Portion of Luck) × (Protest / Loyalty)
    (dropping Intelligence or Willpower depending on terrain, duration, class, and balancing tests). Character B calculates the matching formula, or the total is compared against a fixed target number.
  • XP & Training: XP is spent primarily on buying new skills (skills need not match one's Position; stronger skills are harder to master; players can teach skills to each other bypassing the shop and gaining mutual XP). Roleplaying training always grants minor XP plus +1 to the trained stat (carefully calibrated for episodic play across varying Floors).
  • Skill Power Output: Even though flat attack numbers aren't used, Shinsu skill strength, spatial coverage, and the effort required for a target to survive an attack scale directly with Baang–Surface–Mass and Skill Rank.
